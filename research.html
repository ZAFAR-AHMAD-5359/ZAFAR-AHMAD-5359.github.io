Cross-modal reconstruction of cardiac signals

Heart sounds capture the mechanical consequences of cardiac activity. My thesis investigates whether their relationship with the ECG can provide a useful timing reference for analysing murmurs within the cardiac cycle.

Question
Can acoustic recordings support electrical-timing estimation and phase-aware murmur characterisation?
Method
Conditional generative modelling, cardiac-phase features and comparisons with acoustic baselines.
My contribution
Model development, experiments, evaluation and the first-author manuscripts.

The work studies an inferred timing reference. It does not establish a replacement for measured ECG or a clinically validated diagnostic system.

Evaluation lessons and current direction

Reliable phase information can help describe a murmur. The harder question is whether automatically reconstructed timing adds value for unseen patients. My current evaluation separates these questions using patient-grouped comparisons, in-fold preprocessing and uncertainty estimates. Cross-device reconstruction and end-to-end clinical transfer remain limitations.

The journal revisions take priority over older presentation figures when describing current conclusions. Historical results from different split protocols should not be treated as directly comparable.

03Qatar University collaborationIn development

Since September 2024

Probabilistic models of electromechanical coupling

I am developing copula-based coupled hidden Markov models to connect ECG events with heart-sound phases. Exploratory analysis examines how dependence changes with noise and why event-level alignment is more informative than pooled sample correlation.

Electrical events
P, QRS and T-wave states.
Acoustic phases
S1, systole, S2 and diastole.
Direction
Modelling nonlinear dependence while retaining physiological timing.

04Published conference researchICAI · EUSIPCO

2023 · 2024

Learning from paediatric heart sounds

My first-author conference papers study congenital heart-disease detection and phonocardiogram classification. This work combines time-frequency representations, compact convolutional models and augmentation informed by clinically relevant frequency bands.

Hospital and school recording experience, alongside collaboration with cardiologists, shaped how I approached labels, acquisition quality and model evaluation.

Doctoral direction

Measurements that
explain physiology.

I want to develop the modelling and validation skills needed to move from useful signal features toward interpretable measurements of individual cardiac function.

Electromechanical coupling

Relating electrical events, cardiac sounds and peripheral pulse dynamics to physiology and valvular disease phenotypes.

Personalised models

Connecting digital biomarkers with patient-specific physiological models, with attention to identifiability and uncertainty.

Transfer & validation

Understanding failure across cohorts, devices and recording conditions; designing patient-independent and prospective evaluation.
