Cross-modal reconstruction of cardiac signals

Formal thesis title: “Cross-Modal Reconstruction of Cardiac Signals: Decoding ECG-PCG Electromechanical Coupling Dynamics” (NUST, 2026).

Heart sounds capture the mechanical consequences of cardiac activity. Murmur grading, timing and shape depend on where a sound falls within the cardiac cycle, yet stethoscope-only settings have no simultaneous ECG to mark those phases. My thesis tests whether the heart sound’s relationship with the ECG can provide that timing reference.

Question
Can timing inferred from heart sounds support murmur characterisation for unseen patients?
Method
Conditional signal reconstruction, phase-based features and patient-grouped comparisons with equal-time and acoustic baselines.
My contribution
Developed reconstruction and phase-analysis workflows, designed experiments and patient-grouped evaluation, led the first-author manuscripts and a blind cross-device audit of the reconstruction.

Current finding · October 2026: A re-evaluation kept each patient’s recordings in one fold and fitted preprocessing on training data only. Expert-reviewed phase boundaries carried useful information for murmur characterisation. The tested synthetic-ECG timing route did not establish an advantage over equal-time windows in this patient-grouped dataset evaluation. This separates the value of accurate phase information from the still-unestablished benefit of automatically reconstructed timing.

Next question: Can a reliable automatic timing method preserve that phase information across patients and recording systems? How this leads to my doctoral question

Data roles, evaluation and limits

Paired ECG–PCG recordings support reconstruction development; separate-device paired recordings test reconstruction transfer. The public paediatric CirCor DigiScope heart-sound dataset is used for downstream murmur evaluation, with ten patient-grouped folds, macro-F1 for each task and paired patient-level bootstrap uncertainty. These are distinct data roles, rather than one shared clinical cohort.

The blind cross-device audit covered 338 record pairs from a different recording system. It did not establish successful transfer to that hardware. Expert-reviewed semi-automatic phase annotations are also distinct from an automatic segmenter; no fully automatic direct-PCG segmentation arm was tested in the October evaluation.

Current conclusions use the October 2026 patient-grouped evaluation, which supersedes the earlier thesis and congress analyses; those earlier results should not be cited as evidence for synthetic-ECG timing. Whether an improved or retrained timing method transfers reliably to new devices, and how it would fit a clinical workflow, remains unresolved. An inferred timing reference is not a replacement for measured diagnostic ECG.

Probabilistic models of electromechanical coupling

I am developing copula-based coupled hidden Markov models (HMMs) to relate ECG events to heart-sound phases within each beat. Exploratory analysis tests how dependence changes with noise and whether event-level alignment captures relationships that pooled sample correlation misses.

Implemented so far
Exploratory analysis of synchronous ECG–PCG recordings using spectral, correlation and rank-based dependence measures; the coupled model remains in development.
My role
Develop the dependence analysis and coupled-model formulation, linking P-wave, QRS-complex and T-wave states with the first heart sound (S1), systole, the second heart sound (S2) and diastole.
Next test
Compare event-aligned dependence with pooled sample measures under matched noise and recording conditions.

04Published conference researchICAI · EUSIPCO

2023 · 2024

Learning from heart sounds

My two first-author conference papers address distinct heart-sound classification tasks. They use different datasets, populations and evaluation protocols, so their results are not directly comparable.

ICAI 2023 · Paediatric CHD
I trained a one-dimensional convolutional network on filtered four-second heart-sound segments, with pitch-shift augmentation, for binary normal/abnormal congenital-heart-disease detection from locally collected paediatric recordings and a small public set.
EUSIPCO 2024 · Five-class PCG
I developed band-selective pitch augmentation and a 1D convolutional classification workflow using mel-frequency cepstral coefficients (MFCCs) for a separate valvular/normal heart-sound benchmark.
Clinical acquisition
I developed collection procedures, consent documents and clinical questionnaires, and worked with cardiologists on echocardiography-confirmed annotation.

Hospital and school recording experience shaped my approach to acquisition quality and labels. The published benchmark results do not establish clinical deployment or transfer to an untested cohort.

Doctoral direction

Measurements that
explain physiology.

I bring experience in cardiac acoustic acquisition, signal-model development and patient-grouped evaluation. My modelling so far is statistical and data-driven; during doctoral training I want to learn physiological modelling and prospective validation.

Why this question. In the October 2026 patient-grouped re-evaluation of my thesis work, expert-reviewed cardiac-phase boundaries carried information that equal-time windows missed, while the tested synthetic timing route did not establish an advantage. The wearable work meets the same problem from another side: an ECG-to-pulse delay combines pre-ejection time with pulse travel, so one measured interval cannot separate two physiological quantities without further constraints. The coupled-model work asks how electrical and acoustic events depend on each other within a beat. Each strand asks which physiological quantities measured timing can actually identify, and with what uncertainty.

A question I want to pursue: Can signal-derived cardiac timing constrain patient-specific physiological models, and how do measurement uncertainty and recording-device differences affect identifiability?

Electromechanical coupling

Relating electrical events, cardiac sounds and peripheral pulse dynamics to physiology and valvular disease phenotypes.

Personalised models

Learning how multimodal measurements constrain physiological models, rather than assuming a signal feature uniquely identifies cardiac function.

Transfer & validation

Testing where methods fail across cohorts, devices and recording conditions, through patient-independent and prospective evaluation.
