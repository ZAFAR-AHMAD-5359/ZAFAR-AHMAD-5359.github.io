CARDIAC RESEARCH
The research investigates where cardiac-phase boundaries should come from when a stethoscope recording has no ECG, so that murmur grade, timing and shape can be read in phase. In the October 2026 patient-grouped re-evaluation, expert-reviewed phase boundaries carried useful information, and the thesis’s synthetic-ECG route, with phases fixed at its reconstructed R-peaks, did not separate from equal-time windows. A pre-registered comparison of learnt, acoustic and expert-reviewed boundary sources (analysis plan frozen before any arm was scored) has been completed for the journal revision; its findings will be summarised on the research page once the revision is submitted. Reliable automatic timing across devices and cohorts, and clinical transfer, remain open research questions.
Two first-author journal manuscripts are in revision (IEEE Journal of Biomedical and Health Informatics, second minor revision requested October 2026; European Heart Journal – Digital Health, major revision requested September 2026, revised manuscript prepared). Neither is accepted or published, and no clinical-readiness claim is made. Both ESC Congress 2026 abstracts are published in the European Heart Journal supplement (volume 47, Supplement 1).
