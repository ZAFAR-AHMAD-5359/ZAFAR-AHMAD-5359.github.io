MS thesis: Cross-Modal Reconstruction of Cardiac Signals: Decoding ECG-PCG Electromechanical Coupling Dynamics. Supervisor: Dr. Nazia Perwaiz; co-supervisor: Dr. Muhammad Salman Khan.

Question: Can timing inferred from heart sounds support murmur characterisation for unseen patients?
My contribution: Developed reconstruction and phase-analysis workflows, designed experiments and patient-grouped evaluation, led the first-author manuscripts and a blind cross-device audit of the reconstruction. The audit covered 338 record pairs from different recording hardware; it did not establish successful transfer to that hardware.
Current finding: A re-evaluation in October 2026 kept each patient’s recordings in one fold and fitted preprocessing on training data only. Expert-reviewed cardiac-phase boundaries carried useful information for murmur characterisation. The tested synthetic-ECG timing route did not establish an advantage over equal-time windows. This separates the value of accurate phase information from the unestablished benefit of automatically reconstructed timing.
Method: Each patient’s recordings stay within one evaluation group; preprocessing is fitted on training folds only; methods are compared on matched patient folds with paired uncertainty. The downstream evaluation used the public paediatric CirCor DigiScope dataset with ten patient-grouped folds and macro-F1 for each task. Paired ECG-PCG reconstruction data, separate-device reconstruction checks and paediatric downstream murmur evaluation have different roles.
Next question: Can reliable automatic timing preserve that phase information across patients and recording systems? An inferred timing reference is not a replacement for measured diagnostic ECG. Earlier thesis and congress analyses are superseded by the October evaluation and are not evidence for synthetic-ECG timing.
