MS thesis: Cross-Modal Reconstruction of Cardiac Signals: Decoding ECG-PCG Electromechanical Coupling Dynamics. Supervisors: Nazia Perwaiz and Muhammad Salman Khan.

Question: Can timing inferred from heart sounds support murmur characterisation for unseen patients?
My contribution: Developed reconstruction and phase-analysis workflows, designed experiments and patient-grouped evaluation, led the first-author manuscripts and a blind reconstruction audit across recording devices. The audit covered 338 record pairs; it tested transfer, rather than demonstrating clinical generalisation.
Current lesson: The October 2026 patient-grouped dataset evaluation found useful information in expert-reviewed cardiac-phase boundaries. The tested synthetic-ECG timing route did not establish an advantage over equal-time windows. This separates the value of accurate phase information from the unestablished benefit of automatically reconstructed timing.
Method: Keep each patient's recordings within one evaluation group, fit preprocessing on training folds only, compare methods on matched patient folds and report paired uncertainty. Paired ECG-PCG reconstruction data, separate-device reconstruction checks and paediatric downstream murmur evaluation have different roles.
Next question: Can reliable automatic timing preserve that phase information across patients and recording systems? An inferred timing reference is not a replacement for measured diagnostic ECG. Earlier thesis and congress findings used different protocols and are historical results.
