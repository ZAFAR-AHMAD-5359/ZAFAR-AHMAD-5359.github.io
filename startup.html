Cardiovascular research direction

Heart sounds carry
timing and structure.

In a patient-grouped evaluation, expert-reviewed cardiac-phase boundaries carried useful information for murmur characterisation. The open problem is obtaining that timing automatically from the heart sound itself. The diagram shows the route tested so far: a research concept, not a diagnostic result.

Current finding · October 2026: The tested synthetic-ECG timing route did not establish an advantage over equal-time windows. Whether an automatic timing method can preserve phase information for unseen patients remains open; any clinical use would require separate clinical validation.

Engineering delivery and cardiac research have distinct milestones. Each step follows an agreed task or an evidence-based decision.

Engineering delivery

  1. Define the task

    Identify the recurring problem and agree the intended user, inputs and output.

  2. Agree how success is assessed

    Set acceptance criteria, recording assumptions, platform constraints and sharing boundaries.

  3. Deliver an inspectable workflow

    Provide the agreed outputs, reproducible methods and a concise account of limitations.

  4. Identify repeatable work

    Assess which demonstrated workflow could become a maintained tool.

Cardiac research gates

  1. Find a reliable automatic timing route

    The first tested route did not establish an advantage over equal-time windows. Test whether an automatic timing method can preserve the value of reliable phase boundaries for unseen patients, under the same patient-grouped comparison.

  2. Evaluate transfer and uncertainty

    Test the complete workflow across recording systems and cohorts, including failure cases.

  3. Define a supported application

    Identify a useful research workflow with domain collaborators and establish the additional validation it needs.
