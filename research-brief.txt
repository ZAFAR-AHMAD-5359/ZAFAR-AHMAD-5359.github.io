I am an electrical engineer and cardiovascular signal-processing researcher with an MS from the National University of Sciences and Technology (NUST), conferred on 18 May 2026. I study electrocardiogram (ECG), phonocardiogram or heart-sound (PCG), and photoplethysmogram or optical-pulse (PPG) signals, connecting clinical data collection, deep learning and wearable prototyping, and I ask how far the cardiac timing a recording supplies can be trusted across patients and devices.

MS thesis: Cross-Modal Reconstruction of Cardiac Signals: Decoding ECG-PCG Electromechanical Coupling Dynamics. Supervisor: Dr. Nazia Perwaiz; co-supervisor: Dr. Muhammad Salman Khan.

Question: Where should cardiac-phase boundaries come from when a stethoscope recording has no simultaneous ECG, and how much of the value of expert-reviewed segmentation can an automatic method recover for unseen patients?
My contribution: Developed reconstruction and phase-analysis workflows, designed experiments and patient-grouped evaluation, led the first-author manuscripts and a blind cross-device audit of the reconstruction. The audit covered 338 record pairs recorded with a different stethoscope at a different chest site; agreement did not transfer without retraining, and under matched hardware the reconstruction gives approximate cardiac-cycle timing, not a diagnostic ECG.
Finding to date (October 2026): In the October re-evaluation, with every patient’s recordings kept in one fold, expert-reviewed phase boundaries carried useful information for murmur characterisation, and the thesis’s synthetic-ECG route, with phases fixed at its reconstructed R-peaks, did not separate from equal-time windows. A pre-registered comparison of learnt, acoustic and expert-reviewed boundary sources (analysis plan frozen before any arm was scored; to be released with the paper) was submitted with the EHJ-DH revision (R1, under review) and supersedes that re-evaluation (not accepted or published); its findings will be summarised on the portfolio once the review concludes.
Method: Thesis and the JBHI manuscript: conditional reconstruction of an ECG-like signal from heart sounds. EHJ-DH manuscript (revised October 2026): a pre-registered, participant-grouped comparison of cardiac-phase boundary sources on the public CirCor DigiScope dataset, with one feature extractor, one classifier recipe and one fold split, so that only the boundaries differ; macro-F1 for each task and paired participant-level bootstrap uncertainty. Paired ECG–PCG reconstruction data, separate-device reconstruction checks and the paediatric downstream murmur evaluation have different roles.
What the revision tests: whether phase boundaries learnt from the heart sound alone recover the value of expert-reviewed segmentation, against equal-time windows and an acoustic segmenter, for unseen participants. Earlier thesis and congress analyses are superseded by the pre-registered comparison and are not evidence for synthetic-ECG timing. The timing these methods supply is a phase reference for analysis, not a diagnostic ECG.

Doctoral direction: I work on the measurement side of cardiac deep learning: the cardiac timing recordings can supply, and how far it can be trusted. My thesis asked whether a heart-sound recording can mark its own cardiac phases; a model that worked on one dataset did not transfer to new hardware, and that result set my direction. In a PhD I want to study cardiac-timing models under distribution shift: how a model can tell that a recording, device or population lies outside validated conditions; how much timing error each clinical reading tolerates; and how models stay calibrated as devices and cohorts change. I bring field acquisition, patient-grouped and pre-registered evaluation and cross-device testing, and want to learn continual learning and physiological modelling.
Research interests: cardiac-phase timing from single-sensor recordings; reliability under distribution shift; signal-quality gates for downstream models; wearable ECG–PPG timing; paediatric murmur characterisation. Applying for PhD positions starting in 2027.
