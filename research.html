Cross-modal reconstruction of cardiac signals

MS thesis: Cross-Modal Reconstruction of Cardiac Signals: Decoding ECG-PCG Electromechanical Coupling Dynamics.

Heart sounds capture the mechanical consequences of cardiac activity. My thesis tests whether their relationship with the ECG can provide a timing reference for analysing murmurs within the cardiac cycle.

Question
Can timing inferred from heart sounds support murmur characterisation for unseen patients?
Method
Conditional signal reconstruction, phase-based features and patient-grouped comparisons with equal-time and acoustic baselines.
My contribution
Developed reconstruction and phase-analysis workflows, designed experiments and patient-grouped evaluation, led the first-author manuscripts and a blind reconstruction audit across recording devices.

Current finding · October 2026: Expert-reviewed phase boundaries carried useful information for murmur characterisation. The tested synthetic-ECG timing route did not establish an advantage over equal-time windows in this patient-grouped dataset evaluation. This separates the value of accurate phase information from the still-unestablished benefit of automatically reconstructed timing.

Next question: Can a reliable automatic timing method preserve that phase information across patients and recording systems?

Data roles, evaluation and limits

Paired ECG–PCG recordings support reconstruction development; separate-device paired recordings test reconstruction transfer. The paediatric CirCor DigiScope heart-sound dataset is used for downstream murmur evaluation. These are distinct data roles, rather than one shared clinical cohort.

The blind cross-device audit covered 338 record pairs. It was a test of transfer, not evidence of successful clinical generalisation. Expert-reviewed semi-automatic phase annotations are also distinct from an automatic segmenter; no fully automatic direct-PCG segmentation arm was tested in the October campaign.

Current conclusions use the October 2026 patient-grouped evaluation. Earlier thesis and congress results used different protocols and are historical findings. Reliable transfer to different recording devices and complete clinical workflows remains unresolved. An inferred timing reference is not a replacement for measured diagnostic ECG.

Time-aligned ECG, PPG and motion sensing

Within CAREON (Continuous AI-Remote E-Health Operations Network), a NUST wearable research programme, I lead technical work and supervise three apprentices across firmware, sensor integration, signal processing and the cloud data pipeline.

Implemented so far
Wearable prototype development using an ESP32, ECG and optical pulse sensors, and an inertial measurement unit (IMU); pilot recordings with consenting volunteers.
My role
Set the sampling/acquisition plan, identified analogue-to-digital converter (ADC) precision as a measurement constraint and specified an external-ADC fallback.
Next test
Evaluate timing fidelity and motion sensitivity before interpreting ECG–PPG features across recording conditions.

The ECG-to-peripheral-pulse delay includes pre-ejection time; it should not be treated as isolated pulse transit time. Blood-pressure estimation remains a research goal, and the prototype is not established as a clinically validated monitoring device.

Probabilistic models of electromechanical coupling

I am developing copula-based coupled hidden Markov models (HMMs) to relate ECG events to heart-sound phases within each beat. Exploratory analysis tests how dependence changes with noise and whether event-level alignment captures relationships that pooled sample correlation misses.

Implemented so far
Exploratory analysis of synchronous ECG–PCG recordings using spectral, correlation and rank-based dependence measures; the coupled model remains in development.
My role
Develop the dependence analysis and coupled-model formulation, linking P-wave, QRS-complex and T-wave states with S1, systole, S2 and diastole.
Next test
Compare event-aligned dependence with pooled sample measures under matched noise and recording conditions.

Learning from heart sounds

My two first-author conference papers address distinct heart-sound classification tasks. Their datasets, populations and evaluation protocols should be assessed separately.

ICAI 2023 · Paediatric CHD
I built short-time Fourier and wavelet front-ends and convolutional models for binary congenital-heart-disease detection from paediatric PCG recordings.
EUSIPCO 2024 · Five-class PCG
I developed band-selective pitch augmentation and a 1D convolutional classification workflow using mel-frequency cepstral coefficients (MFCCs) for a separate valvular/normal heart-sound benchmark.
Clinical acquisition
Developed collection procedures, consent documents and clinical questionnaires, and worked with cardiologists on echocardiography-confirmed annotation.

Hospital and school recording experience shaped my approach to acquisition quality and labels. The published benchmark results do not establish clinical deployment or transfer to an untested cohort.

Doctoral direction

explain physiology.

Electromechanical coupling

Relating electrical events, cardiac sounds and peripheral pulse dynamics to physiology and valvular disease phenotypes.

Personalised models

Learning how multimodal measurements constrain physiological models, rather than assuming a signal feature uniquely identifies cardiac function.

Transfer & validation

Testing failure across cohorts, devices and recording conditions through patient-independent and prospective evaluation.
